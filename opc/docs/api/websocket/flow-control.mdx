---
title: "Flow control"
description: "What the server does when a client reads slower than the computer writes."
---

The WebSocket carries responses to your requests and terminal data, nothing else. A session's events and deltas are on [Live](https://docs.opencomputer.dev/api/live), which holds no history: a client that falls behind there reads over [HTTP](/api/http/events) from its cursor.

## Terminal output

A terminal can write faster than a slow connection can carry. Its output goes out in frames of at most 64 KiB, in order, and the terminal is paused while the connection is congested, so a flood of output waits on the computer rather than growing the queue. Nothing is dropped or merged.

## After `4008`

A client that stops reading entirely, so that more than 16 MiB waits on the whole connection, is closed with `4008`. Its terminals are hung up with it. Reconnect, and open new terminals.

## Being a fast client

- **Read the socket on its own.** Parse frames as they arrive and render on a timer, at most once per screen frame.
- **Turn off Nagle's algorithm** (`TCP_NODELAY`) if your library lets you. `opc` and the server do.

## Your sending

| | Limit | Over it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requests | 100 a second per connection, with bursts of 100 | `-32000` with `data.code: "rate_limited"` and `data.retryAfterMs` |
| Message size | 1 MiB | The connection closes with `1009` |
| Terminal input | 1 MiB a second per terminal | As requests |

Compression (`permessage-deflate`) is not offered.
